# Reconstruction: Oral Technology as Instrument Specification

> **Confidence: split, deliberately.** Every physical mechanism cited here
> is documented and citable. The claim that a particular oral tradition was
> encoding *these* mechanisms is inference, and it is labelled as inference
> throughout. The value of the reconstruction is not that it is probably
> right — it is that it is **testable**, mostly on a bench, mostly with
> materials that cost nothing.

This page follows from [02-empirical-audit.md](02-empirical-audit.md),
Claim 5. That audit found that claims reading as mystical were, one changed
variable at a time, describing real instrumentation. This page assembles the
pieces into the instrument they appear to specify, computes what can be
computed, and lists the experiments that would confirm or kill it.

Computation lives in [`precession.py`](precession.py) (stdlib only). Run it:

```bash
python Negentropic/precession.py
```

## The central methodological point

The first-pass audit marked most of these claims FALSE and stopped. That
was a correct answer to the wrong question.

A procedure transmitted orally encodes **what to do**, not **why it
works**. When the "why" is reconstructed by someone outside the tradition —
or generated by a language model, which is where several of these framings
came from — the stated mechanism is a guess layered on top of a procedure.
Refuting the guess says nothing about the procedure.

So the audit needs two passes:

1. Is the stated mechanism right? (Usually no.)
2. Is there *any* mechanism under which the procedure works? (Often yes,
   and often standard physics.)

An audit that returns FALSE on pass 1 and stops has not finished. It has
also, in this case, discarded a working navigational instrument.

## The datum is the pole, not the Bear

This is the load-bearing correction, and it reorganises everything below it.

Great Bear is circumpolar above ~41°N today — Alkaid, at declination
+49.3°, is the limiting star. At glacial-margin latitude the whole
constellation never sets. Of every possible sky target it is therefore the
only one that is simultaneously:

- **available every clear night, all year**, with no seasonal gap;
- **a pointer pair** — Dubhe and Merak extended about five times hits the
  pole;
- and therefore **an indicator of the rotation axis, not of a star**.

You do not align to the Bear. You align to **what the Bear points at**. The
Bear is the finder; the pole is the datum. That distinction is almost
certainly why it is the Bear rather than a brighter or a nearer
constellation — brightness is irrelevant to a finder that never sets, and
what you actually need is two stars whose extension lands on the axis.

### One caveat the reconstruction has to carry

"The Bear never sets" is a true statement about **a latitude and an epoch**,
not about the Bear. Declination is not precession-invariant, and
`precession.py` computes how much it moves:

| Epoch | Alkaid circumpolar above | Never sets at 55°N? |
|---|---|---|
| 2000 CE | 40.7°N | yes |
| 2000 BCE | 19.2°N | yes |
| 6000 BCE | 17.0°N | yes |
| 10 000 BCE | 38.4°N | yes |
| 12 000 BCE | 47.8°N | yes |
| 14 000 BCE | 54.7°N | yes, by 0.3° |

The claim survives at glacial-margin latitudes, which is the result that
matters — but it survives with a margin of under a degree at 14 000 BCE and
a comfortable one at 6000 BCE, when the Bear sat far closer to the pole
than it does now. Any version of this reconstruction that names an epoch
has to check this, and the check is one function call.

### Why a pole-aligned mount is the whole design

Align the home stone's **optic axis to the celestial pole** and the entire
sky's diurnal motion collapses to a single rotation about that one axis.

The consequence for polarisation work is the strongest computational
argument in this reconstruction. Skylight polarisation is centred on the
source, with the maximum-polarisation band 90° from it. In a **horizon
frame**, converting "the band lies here" into "the bearing is there" is
spherical trigonometry with three unknowns. In a **pole-aligned frame** it
reduces to:

- **hour angle** — one angle, uniform, advancing with time;
- **declination** — slow seasonal drift, effectively constant across a
  night.

One rotating angle instead of a spherical problem. That is the difference
between an instrument a trained person can read in the field and a
calculation nobody does in the cold.

And because calcite cleavage reproduces the parent's crystallographic axes
exactly in every fragment, **the carried piece inherits the mount
geometry**. It does not need to talk to the parent. It *is* a copy of the
parent's frame.

## The instrument: a pole-referenced field polarimeter

| Component | Role | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **Calcite** (Iceland spar) | **Analyser.** Birefringent double-image nulling; locates the source under overcast and below the horizon | **DOCUMENTED.** Ropars et al. 2012, *Proc. R. Soc. A*. Accurate to a few degrees. This is the Viking sunstone and it works |
| **Quartz** | **Retarder / rotator.** Optically active — rotates the plane of polarisation by a wavelength-dependent angle | **DOCUMENTED** optics. Role in the assembly is **INFERRED** |
| **Still water** | **Level, latitude zero, polarisation standard, differential baseline** — four jobs, below | **DOCUMENTED** optics. Role **INFERRED** |
| **Home stone** | **Surveyed monument.** Optic axis set on the celestial pole; its tilt from horizontal encodes home latitude | **INFERRED**, but mechanically forced |
| **Great Bear** | **Finder** for the pole, via the Dubhe–Merak pointer | **DOCUMENTED** naked-eye practice |
| **Stars** | Absolute bearing, independent cross-check | Trivially documented |
| **Haidinger's brush** | Unaided human perception of skylight polarisation via macular dichroism. Trainable; most people need to be shown once | **DOCUMENTED**. The instrument has a naked-eye fallback |

### Why the two-mineral combination matters

This is what turns a curiosity into an instrument, and it is the strongest
single argument that the assembly is real rather than assembled by
hindsight:

- **Calcite alone gives you intensity matching.** You compare the
  brightness of two images and null them. The human eye is *poor* at
  absolute brightness matching.
- **Quartz in the path turns the null into a colour transition.** Because
  quartz's optical rotation is wavelength-dependent, the null stops being
  "these two patches are equally bright" and becomes "the colour flips
  here". The eye is *far* better at colour nulling.

That is the operating principle of a laboratory polarimeter. A tradition
specifying both minerals together, rather than either alone, is specifying
the precision upgrade — and there is no reason for a decorative practice to
converge on the one combination that fixes the known weakness of the simple
version.

## Latitude falls out for free

The altitude of the celestial pole above the horizon equals the observer's
latitude. Exactly. No approximation, no table.

So a home stone set on the pole at the home site **encodes home latitude as
its tilt from horizontal**. The field procedure follows mechanically:

1. **Water gives true level.** Without a horizon reference you have no
   zero, and the tilt angle means nothing.
2. **Set the carried fragment on its inherited axis** against the local
   pole.
3. **The angular difference from the home stone's tilt is the latitude
   offset from home** — read directly off the instrument. No tables, no
   arithmetic, no arithmetic done cold and wrong.
4. **The same water surface, viewed at ~53° from vertical, is a
   Brewster-polarised reference of known orientation** — a free calibration
   standard for the analyser.

### Still water does four jobs

The earlier version of this page listed three. Adding the pole datum added
a fourth, and it is the one that makes the instrument a *latitude*
instrument rather than only a bearing instrument:

1. **Artificial horizon.** A level reference. Required for any altitude
   measurement — no level, no latitude.
2. **Latitude zero.** The plane against which the home stone's inherited
   tilt is read.
3. **Brewster reflection.** At ~53° from vertical, water reflects
   near-completely linearly polarised light: a calibration standard of
   known polarisation, free, on site.
4. **Differential baseline.** Compare direct sky against reflected sky.
   Differential nulling beats absolute intensity matching by a large
   factor.

In a glacial environment, unfrozen still water is seasonally scarce. A
tradition that flags it as a precondition for accuracy is tracking an
**instrument requirement**, not expressing a preference. That asymmetry — a
costly, inconvenient requirement stated as mandatory — is what distinguishes
a technical constraint from a ritual one.

## "The home stone must be in place" — closed

First-pass reading: stone-to-stone signalling. Correctly rejected — there is
no channel. **That claim stays dead, and nothing in the reconstruction needs
it.**

The correct reading requires no transmission at all. Calcite cleaves on
fixed lattice planes, so **every fragment of one parent reproduces the
parent's crystallographic axes exactly**, relative to its own cleavage
faces. Therefore:

- Emplace the parent and survey its optic axis onto the celestial pole.
- Its tilt from horizontal now records home latitude.
- Every fragment is a portable copy of that frame.
- If the parent moves, the reference is void and **every fragment in the
  network is invalidated at once**.

"Must be in place" is a surveyed reference monument and a datum-integrity
requirement. No field, no carrier, no channel.

## Precession forces a re-datum protocol

A pole-aligned datum is not stable. The pole drifts about **one degree per
71.6 years**, a full circuit in **25,772 years**:

| Epoch | Pole star | Minimum separation |
|---|---|---|
| 2103 CE | Polaris | 0.46° |
| 2830 BCE | Thuban | 0.20° |
| 11 983 BCE | Vega | 4.83° |

(Computed by `precession.py`; the model reproduces the standard reference
epochs to within a few decades.)

So a datum "oriented to Great Bear" is **not stable across the depth of
time in question**. Either:

- **(a)** the alignment was re-established periodically, or
- **(b)** it drifted, and the tradition should show accumulated error.

**(a) is the survivable branch**, and it has a consequence: there is a
re-alignment practice somewhere in the record. The interval is computable.
`alignment_window` gives the years an alignment holds to a stated tolerance:

| Star | Tolerance | Window |
|---|---|---|
| Polaris | 0.5° | 71 yr |
| Polaris | 1.0° | 317 yr |
| Thuban | 0.5° | 165 yr |
| Thuban | 1.0° | 351 yr |

A half-degree alignment needs re-establishing **every two to five
generations**. That is frequent enough to be a living practice rather than
a lost one, which is what makes branch (a) plausible in the first place.

### Structural note — flagged, not asserted

The re-datum operation is formally identical to `recenter()` in
[`rebase.py`](rebase.py): the base is re-established against current
evidence, every dependent reference reorients, and the old base is retained
as history.

If the re-datum practice exists in the record, NEG-4 stops being an analogy
and becomes the same algorithm at two scales. **This is worth checking. It
is not a claim.** It is recorded here so that if someone does find the
practice, the connection is already written down rather than constructed
after the fact — which is exactly the failure mode this folder exists to
catch.

### Testable dating

This is the part that produces a result from the stories alone.

> If the tradition names a **specific configuration** — a named star at a
> named position at a named season — that alignment was exact in a
> computable year.

Precession dates the tradition to roughly **±100 years** for a tight
alignment. No excavation, no radiocarbon, no site access. `alignment_window`
computes the interval; tighten the tolerance to the precision the
description implies and read the epoch off.

Two cautions. The pole **returns**: any star-at-pole claim is periodic with
a 25,772-year cycle, so the right circuit has to be chosen on other grounds
(Vega is a pole star at both 11 983 BCE and 13 790 CE). And proper motion is
not in the model — over 14,000 years it is the dominant error for anything
in Ursa Major, because Alkaid and Dubhe travel opposite to the other five
Plough stars and the asterism visibly deforms. **Do not use `precession.py`
to evaluate pointer geometry deep in time.** It is correct for the pole and
for single stars.

### "Moon out is better" — why it is closed

Lunar skylight carries the same Rayleigh polarisation pattern as solar, with
the maximum-polarisation band 90° from the source. At glacial latitudes in
winter the sun sits at or below the horizon, putting the band near the
zenith where it is useless for horizon work, while the full moon rides high
and puts the band **at the horizon** — exactly where the sight is taken.

The moon is the better source there for geometric reasons, not preference.
This is a strong confirmation because it is latitude-specific and could not
have been guessed.

### "Sings back" — why it is live rather than dead

The first pass computed **compressional** modes for a chunky stone: kHz–MHz,
far above vocal range, dead. Wrong modes. A thin, elongated or bladed
crystal has **flexural** modes scaling as t/L², landing at **200–800 Hz** —
squarely in vocal range, and felt directly when held.

**Prediction:** the singing stones were bladed or elongated, not chunks.

Separately and at a different scale: a cairn or rock-stack cavity has
Helmholtz and structural modes in the vocal band, and some rock rings
audibly from internal stress (ringing rocks, booming dunes, both
documented). The first pass reduced a described *stack* to a single crystal
and lost the cavity.

## The pitch question, narrowed

Two hypotheses, and the pole datum **weakens one of them**.

**H1 — pitch as data.** The song *is* the storage: bearings, distances and
landmarks encoded as melody. Hand heat is then keeping the crystal
frost-free and thermally stable — mundane, necessary, unremarkable.
*Predicts:* pitch matters for the **song**, not the **stone**.

**H2 — pitch as readout.** A natural uncut quartz element has a large
temperature coefficient of frequency — precisely why watchmakers use
specific cuts to zero it out. An uncut stone is a **thermometer whose
readout is pitch**, and hand heat is the excitation and tuning input. A
navigator wants precise temperature because atmospheric refraction at low
altitude is ~35 arcmin and strongly temperature-dependent, and arctic
conditions produce extreme refraction anomalies — looming, mirage — that
wreck horizon sights. A temperature reading is a **refraction correction**.

**The pole sight weakens H2.** At 60°N the pole sits 60° above the horizon,
where refraction is about **0.5 arcmin** — no correction needed, and no
reason to carry a thermometer for it. So if pitch mattered at all, it
mattered for the **horizon polarisation sight**, not for the pole sight.

That sharpens the discriminator rather than settling it: H2 survives only in
the restricted form "temperature correction for low-altitude work", which is
a narrower and more falsifiable claim than the original. Also still open
under H2: hand heat shifts calcite birefringence measurably, so warming the
analyser is a fine-tune on retardance.

## The scent protocol

Spruce is a good candidate for the carrier, and it is mechanically
load-bearing rather than decorative:

- **Resin as fixative.** Spruce resin slows volatile release. The protocol
  requires a scent held at controlled strength over days with graded
  crossover, and a raw odorant cannot do that — it flashes off. A resin
  fixative is exactly the missing piece that makes duration-as-dose
  physically possible.
- **Bornyl acetate / α-pinene** are strong, persistent, and — critically —
  chemically *adjacent* to other conifer volatiles. The stated rule was that
  the replacement scent must be similar, never jarring. A conifer base
  supplies a family of near neighbours to step through.

So spruce is plausibly **both** the carrier and the substitution ladder.

**Safety bound, restated because it is independent of all of the above.**
Thujone is convulsant at dose; *Tanacetum vulgare* is hepatotoxic and
abortifacient. A corrected mechanism is not a licence to attempt the
protocol — a coherent-sounding rationale is exactly what would persuade
someone to try it. The arousal-destabilisation hypothesis is testable with
manipulations that are not hepatotoxic; the plant is load-bearing only for
the historical reconstruction, never for the mechanism. See
[02-empirical-audit.md](02-empirical-audit.md).

## Discriminating tests

Ordered by how cheaply they settle something. T5 needs no field access at
all.

| ID | Test | Resolves |
|----|------|----------|
| **T1** | Is the carried stone **bladed/elongated** or **chunky**? | Bladed → flexural modes in vocal range → the hum is solved |
| **T2** | Does the clear stone **double an image**? | Yes → calcite → the whole polarimeter reconstruction stands |
| **T3** | Was accurate pitch needed for the **song**, or to **match the stone** — and if the latter, was it used for **horizon** sights or **pole** sights? | H1 vs H2. The pole sight needs no refraction correction, so H2 survives only for low-altitude work |
| **T4** | Was the home stone **oriented deliberately** when emplaced — and was it **tilted**, or set level? | Tilted and aimed → pole datum + latitude encoding, both confirmed at once |
| **T5** | **Six orderings** of calcite / quartz / water. Which produces a colour null? | The lost sequence. A bench test, not an archaeology question |
| **T6** | Does the tradition aim **at the Bear**, or **past the pointers**? | *At the Bear* → the datum is a star, drifts with precession, needs re-aiming every few generations — and the re-aiming interval is itself a recoverable number. *Past the pointers* → the datum is the pole, precession-robust in concept, but pointer geometry still degrades and still needs periodic check |
| **T7** | Does the record contain a **re-alignment practice**, and does it name a **specific configuration**? | Confirms branch (a) of the precession argument, and dates the tradition to ±100 yr |

T1, T2 and T4 are questions about physical objects. T3, T6 and T7 are
questions for a knowledge holder. T5 is an afternoon with three components.

**What would kill the reconstruction:** T2 returning "no double image"
removes the analyser and the polarimeter account collapses to nothing. T5
finding that no ordering produces a usable colour null removes the reason
for two minerals rather than one. T4 finding the home stone was set level
and unaimed removes the pole datum and takes the latitude encoding with it.
Any of those outcomes should be recorded here as plainly as a confirmation
would be.

## What this page is not

It is not a claim about what any particular people knew. It is a claim that
**a working instrument is consistent with the transmitted procedure**, that
the instrument's components are individually documented, and that seven
specific observations would confirm or refute it.

The failure mode to guard against is the one this whole folder exists to
catch: a reconstruction that explains everything, is confirmed by nothing,
and grows a new epicycle each time a detail does not fit. Each row above is
attached to a test. Rows that fail their test get struck, not amended.

Two things on this page are already qualified rather than confirmed, and
should stay that way until tested: the epoch-dependence of the Bear's
circumpolarity narrows the window in which the reconstruction can be set,
and the pole sight's freedom from refraction narrows H2 to low-altitude
work only. Both are cases of the reconstruction getting *smaller* under
computation. That is the direction a real one should move.
